This is a web page.
There's not much here.
Just words.
We've become obsessed with fancy designs, responsive layouts, and scripts that do magical things.
I wrote these words, and you're reading them: magical.
I'm in a little city in British Columbia; you're probably somewhere else. I wrote this early in the morning, June 20th, 2013; you're reading it at a different time. I wrote this on my laptop; you could be reading this on your phone, a tablet or a desktop.
You and I have been able to connect because and . Despite our different locations, devices, and time-zones we can connect on a simple HTML page.
I wrote this in a text editor. It's 6 kB. I didn't need a Content Management System, a graphic designer, or a software developer. There's not much code on this page at all: just simple markup for paragraphs, hierarchy, and emphasis.
I remember teaching my daughter to code HTML when she was 8. The first thing she wrote was . She wasn't "writing HTML"; . She couldn't believe that she could write a story on our home computer, and then publish it for the world to see. She didn't care about HTML; she cared about sharing her stories.
a story about a squirrel
Think about all the things you could communicate with a simple page like this. If you're a businessperson, you could sell something. If you're a teacher, you could teach something. If you're an artist, you could show something you've made.
If you're a web designer, I challenge you to think about the words first. Instead of starting with a style guide or a Photoshop mockup, .
What do you have to say? If you don't know, there's not much use in adding all that other cruft. , with a single focus. Write it and publish it, and then . Every time you're about to add something, ask yourself: does this help me communicate better? Will that additional styling, image, or hyperlink give my audience more understanding? If the answer is "no," don't add it.
. Words don't come the design is done. Words are the beginning, the core, the focus.
Start with words.
